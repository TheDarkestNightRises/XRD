# Personal Reflections #

## Emanuel-Dan Acalfoaie (315215) Personal reflection
Although I had prior experience with Unity from other projects, this was my first time exploring these specific areas. I thoroughly enjoyed the process, especially creating the two projects we worked on. <br>
First the augmented reality project that we built represented a history portal where you can travel through different areas of time. Using the Unity AR kit, it was wonderful to use the possibilities of the technology to simulate a virtual reality where the player can walk it into. To create the portal, I developed multiple stencil shaders that occlude pixels when the camera collides with the portal. Unfortunately, this solution has a couple of hiccups because the AR camera’s movement is not smooth and that can create clipping with the portal. I didn’t find a good replacement for this solution, most people using a similar technique to achieve this effect in AR. For the environment, I made a Viking village where the player can explore different small houses and villages. The skybox was made using a quad and displaying it over the camera boundary, simulating a nice sunset. Overall, I am pleased to see the effect in notion and as a first AR project this seems a successful and interesting project. <br>
Inspired by the game Superhot, we developed a close replica to the game using the Unity VR. The game was fun to develop and create. I developed enemy AI and was able to create a similar feeling on how the enemy behaved in the original game. The gun throw required some serious physics and documenting myself on velocity formulas. I noticed that the rotation of the characters greatly affects how the game behaved on each gun throw. After a couple of unsuccessful implementations, I managed to create a satisfying gun throw. The environment that I created, the warehouse, was inspired by the first levels of Superhot using a dark and gritty industrial complex with white paint over. It was also fun to develop the chopping mechanic of the enemies, using both Blender and Unity to achieve a satisfying effect while killing enemies. The time slow mechanic was a cool mechanic to implement, and this was done using unity timescale property. Unfortunately, the version of the Unity that we were using has performance issues when scaling the time continuously. The action that is supposed to bring the timescale back to its normal value hangs the main thread, making the game slower and slower. This creates an unpleasant effect when playing a longer gaming session or playing on older hardware. To combat this effect, I used all the textbook techniques to improve performance like mapping shadows, graphics quality, occlusion and shaders. While the game is not as performant as it could be, it is a fun project, and it is close to the original game, being a tremendous opportunity to learn. <br>
Exploring Unity's approach to AR and VR development has been an valuable experience. While the technology shows potential, it can be frustrating at times due to limited documentation and certain limitations. Unity's recent major overhaul to its engine have introduced both opportunities and challenges. Overall, both projects showcased the power and potential of Unity for creating engaging AR and VR experiences. They not only reinforced my understanding of the platform but also inspired me to continue exploring and innovating in this exciting field.

## Andrei George Lichi (315262) Personal Reflection ##

It was my first time working with VR and AR templates in Unity and the technologies that come with them, and I learned a lot of new and interesting things. The whole process required a lot of research to get familiar with the unique aspects of VR and AR such as hand tracking and gestures, grab points, spatial anchors, and many more. I must admit that it was quite hard to find some examples or documentation for most of the stuff that we have implemented since it was either deprecated or not fully compatible. For the AR app, I found the idea of a portal interesting because looking from outside of the box, there were a lot of questions about how the world inside of the portal is hidden from the real world. There are indeed more ways of achieving that, but we decided to use Unity's Stencil Buffer because it was more efficient and flexible since we could control which parts of the scene were rendered. As for my contribution to the AR application, besides the core functionalities, I have worked on the “Mesozoic era” entire environment along with object descriptions display. Being a fan of the “superhot” game, working towards implementing a game like that one was an overall fun experience. It took a lot of time to correctly implement the slowing down of time to match the original game and to create a smooth experience. The game itself was not the hardest part to create but making it work smoothly and creating good level design was the tough one. Apparently “messing up with time has consequences” is not only a quote in the movies, but also in Unity. Slowing down time while several actions such as spawning or destroying objects were happening caused concurrency issues between threads which over time decreased the framer rate until the game became unplayable. It was an annoying bug for which we spent a lot of time, and we just managed to improve it a little. For this VR application, I also worked on different kinds of weapons. I have implemented a katana, which was made not only for combat but also for destroying enemies' bullets, and an ak47 which I used to grab points to mimic the real-life handle of this weapon. The default XR interactable component doesn’t have a way of holding a two-hand weapon from different perspectives such as left or right. So I designed the AK47 to only be held with the left arm on the back of it and the right arm on the front. The spray of bullets coming from the AK47 also needed to be adjusted to be smooth and properly work with the slowing down of time. I also worked on 3 different level designs which I took inspiration from some popular Marvel movies or John Wick. In the end, I was satisfied with what my team and I managed to develop and even though I know some things could’ve been better or improved, the final VR game was fun to play. A VR game has its own unique experience which makes even underdeveloped games an enjoyable experience. As for the hardest part of the entire process, for me, it was the lack of proper documentation, but it was expected since these technologies are still new and not polished enough. But because of that the entire development process was slower than expected and I also personally ran into multiple bugs. 

## Oriana Cimino Amaddeo (315205) Personal Reflection ##
Looking back at the two projects that I did for the XRD subject, I surely got a better understanding of the practical possibilities of both VR and AR. In the project on AR, I designed a virtual museum featuring Leonardo Da Vinci's work as an engineer. That was to allow the users to experience his inventions in new and attractive ways in an immersive environment. What I enjoyed the most was the UI design. I liked the feature I made about how text popped up when the users interacted with objects and disappeared when the camera moved away from them. It felt very natural, like actually looking at the objects and pointing your attention at them, making the experience smooth and intuitive. The challenge is that, in AR it's hard not to clutter with the environment since the merging of real and virtual is hard; immersion requires well-ordered virtual elements that should not overwhelm the user. Finding this balance about, information without overloading through the interactive text, was a big challenge and that is why the whole project is so rewarding. AR balances the music with reality. The loud music would distract one from being around their surroundings. I tried to solve this problem by having the music fade in the moment the user stepped through the portal leading into the Leonardo Da Vinci museum room making it an even more smooth and immersive experience. What impressed me was how AR can change the way we learn. It is not just about a space in virtuality, but also a place where interactivity regarding education comes to life. AR makes learning come alive, especially for a younger audience interacting with history by setting it in 3D. AR can make complex ideas more tangible and exciting. In our VR project, SuperNot, I was able to choose my environments and weapons and was able to create some interactive fighting tools. It was very rewarding to see this game from a concept into creation. We did have the option to create something with relatively complex mechanics since it would be required to loop and handle time within its virtual space. First of all, the user's feelings while playing, the rooms, the interactive weapons, and gameplay were cool. In VR games, the limitation of the players' movement provides better immersion by not disorienting them. So we designed a more controlled environment for players by building a playing area with a set boundary. This reduces motion sickness by keeping players inside that delimited area. The traditional UI menu just doesn't work in VR, and the interactions are pretty clunky. We solved this with the use of hand gestures with VR controllers. This lets the player point and use natural hand movements to navigate through the menu. It is an intuitive approach that makes for a much smoother experience with haptic feedback and is way more immersive. While AR allowed me to explore new methods of presenting information and interacting with it, VR gave me the chance to explore pure creativity and 3D technology. Now, looking back, I value how much I learned in both and am excited about the endless possibilities both AR and VR can have for the future. It was an enriching experience to work on these projects with my colleagues. We brainstormed solutions and shared ideas. Working on the portal, and the SuperNot game together with dynamic teamwork, made that all worth it. I feel grateful for the opportunity to learn from each other and to grow together within these projects.
