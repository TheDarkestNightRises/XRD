# Personal Reflections #


## Andrei George Lichi (315262) Personal Reflection ##

It was my first time working with VR and AR templates in Unity and the technologies that come with them, and I learned a lot of new and interesting things. The whole process required a lot of research to get familiar with the unique aspects of VR and AR such as hand tracking and gestures, grab points, spatial anchors, and many more. I must admit that it was quite hard to find some examples or documentation for most of the stuff that we have implemented since it was either deprecated or not fully compatible. For the AR app, I found the idea of a portal interesting because looking from outside of the box, there were a lot of questions about how the world inside of the portal is hidden from the real world. There are indeed more ways of achieving that, but we decided to use Unity's Stencil Buffer because it was more efficient and flexible since we could control which parts of the scene were rendered. As for my contribution to the AR application, besides the core functionalities, I have worked on the “Mesozoic era” entire environment along with object descriptions display. Being a fan of the “superhot” game, working towards implementing a game like that one was an overall fun experience. It took a lot of time to correctly implement the slowing down of time to match the original game and to create a smooth experience. The game itself was not the hardest part to create but making it work smoothly and creating good level design was the tough one. Apparently “messing up with time has consequences” is not only a quote in the movies, but also in Unity. Slowing down time while several actions such as spawning or destroying objects were happening caused concurrency issues between threads which over time decreased the framer rate until the game became unplayable. It was an annoying bug for which we spent a lot of time, and we just managed to improve it a little. For this VR application, I also worked on different kinds of weapons. I have implemented a katana, which was made not only for combat but also for destroying enemies' bullets, and an ak47 which I used to grab points to mimic the real-life handle of this weapon. In the end, I was satisfied with what my team and I managed to develop and even though I know some things could’ve been better or improved, the final VR game was fun to play. As for the hardest part of the entire process, for me, it was the lack of proper documentation, but it was expected since these technologies are still new and not polished enough.
